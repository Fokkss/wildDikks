1. The Output vs Velocity plots somewhat resemgle the sigmid
function. Low speeds contruibute to low output values; near
the cut-in speed (bottom tail of the S-curve) wind velocity
is low and is insufficient to produce a large power output,
once the cut-in speed is reached (approx. 3 m/s), the power
output increases. Kinetic energy scales as the cube of its
velocity, thus the growth rate is significant. The S-curve
bend represents the turbine's active controll systems
pitching the blades in a specific way aiming at expelling
the excess wind to
prevent mechanical damage. The S-curve top tail represents
the "turbine saturation state" -- the power output remains
constant: no more energy can be extracted from the wind
without undertaking significant mechanical damage.

2. Output-vs-Direction plots seem to have two accumulation
points. This can be considered as a less 
convenient way of representing the wind rose. The model 
is obviously "smart enough" to account for that automatically. 
Just an interesting observation.

3. The Output vs Temperature plots have two interesting
characteristics: it has an accumulation point near 0 deg C
with a high power output; the power output drops with high
temperature, which might point to some hardware
restrictions, e.g. the transformer is uneble to operate at
high temperatures with nominal power conversion coefficient
